# Chemistry

## Coordination thermodynamics

**FACT · REPORTED:** In a 1985 paper with V. K. Patel, Bhattacharya determined formation constants for ternary copper systems in 1:1 dioxane-water at 30 °C and examined how substitution on a catecholate ligand affected stability. [C007]

**FACT · REPORTED:** A second 1985 paper compared ternary nickel-complex stability with the corresponding copper systems as part of an attempt to explain metal selection in biological coordination environments. [C008]

**CONTEXT · FACT · REPORTED:** The nickel paper’s title asked why Ni(II) was excluded from metalloenzymes. Later literature described several nickel-dependent enzymes, so that wording must be presented as the historical framing of the 1985 study, not as current scientific consensus. [C014]

## Schiff-base complexes and epoxidation

**FACT · REPORTED:** The 1989 study used mono- and binuclear iron(III) Schiff-base chelates to catalyze alkene epoxidation with iodosylbenzene. [C009] For norbornene, the paper reported better performance from the binuclear complexes than from the mononuclear set. [C010]

**FACT · REPORTED:** Bhattacharya’s 1990 paper continued the subject explicitly as homogeneous olefin-epoxidation catalysis by mono- and binuclear Schiff-base complexes. [C011] It reported oxidation of various olefins using Mn(III) Schiff-base complexes and binuclear Mn(II) and Fe(III) Schiff-base complexes as catalysts. [C017] The paper correlated catalytic activity with ligand structure, metal-ion redox potential, and binuclear character. [C018]

## Technical interpretation

**INFERENCE:** Taken together, these records show a research arc from measuring how ligand substitution changes metal-complex stability to testing how metal center arrangement changes catalytic behavior. [C015]

**INFERENCE:** The modern bridge is catalyst design: ligand identity, coordination environment, and the use of one versus two metal centers are controllable variables for tuning reactivity. [C016]
