# Depth ladder

## 1. Icon + one sentence

⚗️ **He studied how the structure around a metal can change the chemistry it performs.** [C007–C011]

## 2. ⚡ 30-second

**FACT:** At M.S. University of Baroda, Pabitra Krishna Bhattacharya worked on metal complexes: molecules in which a metal is held by surrounding chemical groups. [C001, C005] His papers measured how stable some of these complexes were and tested iron complexes as catalysts for converting alkenes into epoxides. [C007–C011]

**INFERENCE:** In plain language, he was studying how to redesign a metal’s chemical “neighbourhood” so that its behaviour changes. [C016]

## 3. 📖 3-minute

**FACT:** Bhattacharya’s selected papers span two connected tasks. One task was measurement: determining formation constants for ternary metal complexes and comparing how ligand substitution or metal choice changed stability. [C007, C008] The other was reaction testing: using mono- and binuclear iron(III) Schiff-base chelates to catalyze alkene epoxidation. [C009–C011]

**FACT:** The 1989 work reported that the binuclear complexes performed better than the mononuclear set for norbornene under the study’s conditions. [C010]

**INFERENCE:** The connecting idea is that chemical function is engineered through coordination environment. [C015, C016]

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** This audit found no reliable source explaining why Bhattacharya is not more widely known. It therefore does not manufacture a story of neglect, discrimination, or lost credit.

## 4. 🔬 Research layer

The 1985 copper study determined formation constants for ternary systems in 1:1 dioxane-water at 30 °C and examined catecholate-ring substitution. [C007] A related nickel study compared ternary nickel and copper complex stability in a bioinorganic framing. [C008] The 1989 catalysis paper moved from equilibrium behavior to chemical transformation: mono- and binuclear Fe(III) Schiff-base chelates catalyzed alkene epoxidation with iodosylbenzene, with a reported binuclear advantage for norbornene. [C009, C010] A 1990 paper continued the homogeneous epoxidation theme. [C011]

**INFERENCE:** Read together, these works connect ligand effects, metal choice, nuclearity, complex stability, and oxidation catalysis. [C015, C016]
