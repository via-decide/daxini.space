Pabitra Krishna
Bhattacharya

Professor & Head of Chemistry — M.S. University of Baroda

He studied how the structure around a metal can change the chemistry it performs. Working in Gujarat through the 1970s–2000s, he measured metal complex stability, tested iron–Schiff-base systems as epoxidation catalysts, and co-authored a standard biochemistry text still in print today.

Depth Ladder
⚡ 30-Second
At M.S. University of Baroda, Bhattacharya worked on metal complexes—molecules in which a metal is held by surrounding chemical groups. His papers measured how stable some of these complexes were and tested iron complexes as catalysts for converting alkenes into epoxides. In plain language: he was studying how to redesign a metal's chemical “neighbourhood” so that its behaviour changes.
📖 3-Minute
Bhattacharya's selected papers span two connected tasks.

Measurement: determining formation constants for ternary metal complexes and comparing how ligand substitution or metal choice changed stability. The 1985 copper study determined formation constants for ternary systems in 1:1 dioxane–water at 30 °C. A related nickel study compared ternary nickel and copper complex stability in a bioinorganic framing.

Reaction testing: using mono- and binuclear iron(III) Schiff-base chelates to catalyse alkene epoxidation. The 1989 work reported that the binuclear complexes performed better than the mononuclear set for norbornene.

The connecting idea is that chemical function is engineered through coordination environment.
🔬 Research Layer

These works together connect ligand effects, metal choice, nuclearity, complex stability, and oxidation catalysis.
